## A WORD ABOUT GRANTS (honest-expectations block)

**We prepare and submit. Funders decide.**

Some agencies advertise a "success rate" for Google Ad Grant work. We won't do that, because it's easy to blur the difference between *submitting a well-built application* and *getting approved* — and only the funder controls the second part.

Here's what we can tell you plainly instead:

- **No grant is guaranteed.** Approval decisions belong to the funder, and applications — especially the Google Ad Grant — often take multiple rounds of revisions to your website and materials.
- **It takes real hours.** A 20-hour/month plan is part-time support; grant readiness on that plan is a 3–6 month journey. Larger plans move faster.
- **The upside is real.** When a grant is awarded, our implementation work can often be written into the grant budget as a vendor line item — meaning the grant, not your general fund, pays for the work.

We'd rather set honest expectations now than surprise you later.

## What changed from v1, and why

- **Split the single CTA into two intent-based buttons** (Admin/Ops vs. Grant-Ready), matching what both BELAY and Getting Attention do — it's a structural change, not a claim, so it carries no honesty risk.
- **Added an explicit "we publish our pricing" line.** This is simply true (the table is right there) and turns a passive fact into a stated differentiator, since neither competitor discloses pricing.
- **Replaced vague trust language with one verifiable claim:** "since April 2026" and the real client categories (churches, youth sports, ministries, PTAs, childcare) — no invented client count, no stat that isn't backed by something real.
- **Sharpened the grants section** to name *why* we don't quote a success rate, turning the competitor's ambiguity (Getting Attention's unclear "100%") into an explicit point of contrast — this is honest because it's true PWA hasn't claimed and won't claim an approval rate.
- **Did not add:** a named/trademarked guarantee, any percentage stat, a client logo wall, or testimonial copy — none of these are real yet, so none went on the page. The two-week adjustment idea is included but flagged as unconfirmed policy rather than stated as settled fact.
